Part 7: Stepping Away, Letting the Noise Settle, and Trying to Figure Out What Actually Matters

When I left Cisco, it did not feel triumphant. It felt uncertain. In some ways, it even felt embarrassing.

For most of my adult life, I had always had the next thing lined up. Leaving removed all of that at once.

The First Real Pause

For the first time since graduating from the University of Calgary, I didn’t have a clear next step. No job lined up. No immediate role. Just space.

At first, that space felt uncomfortable. Without a backlog, a deadline, a team, or a manager, the silence was loud.

Looking Back Honestly

Once I stepped away, I could really look back: why did I keep making the choices I made, what patterns repeated, what energized me, what drained me, what did I keep avoiding?

The more I sat with those questions, the more a pattern emerged.

Following Signals Instead of Myself

For most of my career, when I didn’t know what to do, I looked outward. I looked for the confident voice, the strong company, the smart team, the right next move.

Again and again, I outsourced the question of direction. That kept my career moving, but it did not help me build a life that felt truly aligned.

Fear Was Underneath

The more honestly I looked, the clearer it became that fear shaped more of my career than I wanted to admit: fear of being wrong, failing visibly, being exposed, or choosing something unconventional.

Fear made caution feel like wisdom. Sometimes it was. Often it was just fear.

Meditation Changed Meaning

By this point, meditation was no longer a side interest. With more space, the lessons of meditation became more lived and integrated.

I could see how quickly the mind creates a story: this is who I am, this is dangerous, this is failure, this is success, this is the right path.

AI Started to Feel Like a Pull

Around this time, I became increasingly drawn toward artificial intelligence. At first it was curiosity. Then it felt meaningful.

AI seemed to open the door to the kind of work I had wanted for a long time: learning, health, decision-making, governance, and systems that help people navigate complexity.

What Matters Now

I am not only interested in building software. I am interested in solving meaningful problems, making complex systems understandable, working with people, reducing confusion, and contributing to something that matters.

I still care deeply about technology, but I no longer believe the only meaningful way I can contribute is by sitting alone writing code for hours.

What Comes Next

There are experts. Real ones. There are also people who perform expertise. There are leaders who create clarity and leaders who create the appearance of clarity.

It took me a long time to start seeing the difference between what the world rewards and what actually matters to me. But I see it now. And now that I do, I can’t really go back to pretending I don’t.
