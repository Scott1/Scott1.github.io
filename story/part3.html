Part 3: Surrounded by Experts, Afraid to Speak, and Learning in Silence

When I left Symantec for Immunet, I thought I was moving toward something more modern, more exciting, and more meaningful.

Almost immediately, I realized I had stepped into a completely different world.

A Different Kind of Team

At Immunet, the feeling was entirely different. This was the first time in my career where I was surrounded by people I would now confidently describe as real experts.

They weren’t just confident. They weren’t just good presenters. They actually knew what they were doing.

When several of them got into a discussion, it was impressive to watch. They disagreed openly, tested ideas, refined things in real time, and moved from uncertainty to working direction incredibly quickly.

A Completely New Technical World

Up until that point, my technical world had been fairly consistent. I was a Windows developer. Immunet was not that world.

The culture of the team was rooted much more in Linux, Unix, and macOS. I still needed Windows for the client, but almost everything around me lived in a different ecosystem.

I had to become more comfortable with SSH, SCP, command-line workflows, shell tools like grep, sed, and awk, and scripting in environments that felt unfamiliar.

This Was the First Time Security Was Really the Work

At TELUS, security had barely entered the picture. At Symantec, I had worked for a security company, but not really in the heart of its security work. Immunet changed that completely.

Now the product wasn’t just software. It was security software. Its reason for existing was to detect threats, stop them, report on them, and protect machines in real time.

I had to learn a new domain: detections, false positives, file dispositions, signatures, detection engines, telemetry, malware analysis, and event reporting.

Learning Without Admitting How Much I Didn’t Know

I was surrounded by people who clearly had deep expertise, and instead of saying, “I don’t know this yet — can you help me understand it?” I stayed quiet.

I watched. I listened. I tried to piece things together on my own. Part of that was fear, and part of it was the pace of the environment.

Startup Culture at Its Best

Another thing that made that period special was how close we were to the work itself. The VP who talked to customers and sales sat a desk or two away from me.

That kind of proximity makes the work feel real. When you understand why a feature matters and who it matters to, the work has energy.

Finding Meditation

Around this same time, I started searching for something. Somehow I came across Aaron Swartz’s writing, and in one post he mentioned mindfulness meditation.

I didn’t know what it meant, but something about it caught me. Eventually I tried it. It felt strange and awkward, and I didn’t feel good at it, but something had started.

What I Took With Me

Immunet taught me how much I could grow when pushed into an environment that demanded more than I thought I could give.

It also showed me what true expertise looked like. Experts were real. But I was too afraid to fully learn from them.
